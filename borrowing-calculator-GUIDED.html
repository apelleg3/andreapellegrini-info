Step 6: Could you reduce or cancel borrowing?

If you do not need all your loan funds, consider reducing an upcoming disbursement or cancelling part of a recent one. Less principal means less interest.

For federal Direct Loans, funds returned within 120 days of each disbursement can be treated as a cancellation, adjusting the related loan fee and interest. The deadline is not the end of the semester or year. Ask your school or servicer how to process it. Federal loan cancellation guidance.

Reduced borrowing is treated as removed from the original principal, with no early interest payments. For Tiered Standard, the term is recalculated if your total principal falls into a lower tier. Origination-fee savings are not included.

Step 7: What if you pay more each month?

Even with a 25-year Tiered Standard term, you can pay more than the required monthly amount. Extra monthly payments can shorten payoff and reduce interest. Federal student loans have no prepayment penalty. Extra-payment guidance.

How this estimate works
  • Interest accrues daily: outstanding principal × (annual rate ÷ 365.25) × actual days.
  • The pay-interest strategy pays all accrued interest on each monthly date during school and grace. Waiting makes no early payments. The reduced-borrowing comparison also makes no early payments, with a smaller starting principal.
  • During repayment, interest is computed for the actual days between the repayment start and each calendar-month payment date. Payments cover accrued interest first, then principal. A fixed payment is estimated to clear the balance over the term entered.
  • At specified events, such as the end of an unsubsidized loan’s deferment, unpaid interest may be capitalized (added to principal). Future interest then accrues on that larger principal. Loan rules determine these events; borrowers do not choose them. This calculator keeps unpaid interest separate and excludes capitalization events, so it may understate costs when they apply. Federal Student Aid: When does interest capitalize?
  • No origination fees, variable rates, rate discounts, subsidies, taxes, forgiveness, missed payments, or additional borrowing are modeled. This is an educational comparison, not a simulation of RAP or a guarantee of an official repayment-plan payment.
  • The original Standard comparison uses 10 years for nonconsolidated student Direct Loans. Tiered Standard uses total outstanding Direct Loan principal at plan entry: below $25,000 = 10 years; $25,000–below $50,000 = 15; $50,000–below $100,000 = 20; $100,000 or more = 25. Reducing principal may change the tier. Unknown or other loan types use an explicitly entered term.
  • Reduced borrowing represents an amount never disbursed or properly cancelled with its associated interest adjustment. Returning funds outside applicable cancellation deadlines is a payment, not the cancellation modeled here. Origination-fee adjustments are not included in the totals.
