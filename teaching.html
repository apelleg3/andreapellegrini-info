Teaching

Teaching & Learning Design

I teach as a facilitator and mentor, grounded in constructivist learning theory and cognitive science. Most of my teaching is informal: supporting self-regulated learning for adults and students who choose to participate, through live webinars, online learning campaigns, a podcast, and the student interns I supervise.

Approach

Teaching Philosophy

Students learn best when they actively construct knowledge through experiences that combine multimodal resources with the chance to apply that knowledge to a real situation. Because most of my learners choose to participate, I design for intrinsic motivation and self-regulated learning. My training in human resource development and in the Cognitive Science of Teaching & Learning shapes how I do it.

Role

Facilitator & Co-Investigator

I co-construct knowledge with learners rather than transmit fixed answers, treating their questions, experiences, and instincts as legitimate intellectual resources.

Experience

Structured Experiential Learning

I have supervised more than 150 student interns and employees through hands-on projects tied to real institutional needs, weekly training on research methods and professional skills, and reflective deliverables such as written reports, process reflections, and public-facing content. When learners design for a real audience, the stakes change, and that accountability supports learning that transfers.

Design

Multimodal & Accessible Delivery

My webinars, self-paced modules, and podcast episodes combine video, narrative text, embedded visualizations, quizzes, and interactive elements. As my unit's IT Accessibility Liaison, I build materials to meet and exceed WCAG standards, and I treat accessibility as an equity question: whose voice a system was built to carry, and whose it excludes.

AI & Ethics

AI as Tool and Ethical Problem

I teach generative AI as both a tool and a subject of critical inquiry: whose disclosure it invites, what it does to trust, and what consent means when the one asking questions is not human. I also sequence it deliberately, so learners build foundational skills before AI enters the work and the tool extends capability rather than replacing thinking.

Assessment

Aligned, Formative, Hard to Shortcut

Low-stakes quizzes, peer feedback, drafts, stakeholder presentations, and reflection track metacognitive growth alongside content mastery. Because AI can produce a polished artifact with little learner effort, I also assess process documentation, oral defense of design decisions, and in-person critique.
